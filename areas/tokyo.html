A city in concentric rings

Tokyo

Some central shrines are within walking distance. Others sit an hour away by train, or half a day out in the mountains and by the sea. Tokyo is not one point. It spreads in concentric rings. Longer stays make the outer rings possible.

1

Lv1 · Walkable (inside the Yamanote loop)

Within walking distance of central stations. Two or three shrines can fit into one day.

J
Jingu Meiji Jingu Meiji Jingu

The forest covers 700,000 square metres. Every tree was planted by hand when the shrine was founded in 1920. Ten minutes from Harajuku, and the city goes quiet.
N
Notable shrine Kanda Jinja (Kanda Myojin) Kanda Myojin

Guardian shrine of Edo. It enshrines Taira no Masakado, who was defeated by the imperial court and later worshipped as a god. Akihabara is only minutes away.
N
Notable shrine Hie Jinja Hie Jinja

The tutelary shrine of Edo Castle, now the Imperial Palace. Its Sanno Matsuri is one of Japan's three great festivals. An escalator reaches the shrine buildings.
N
Notable shrine Toranomon Kotohira-gu Toranomon Kotohiragu

The shrine buildings are built into the foot of a high-rise office block. The gods are associated with safety at sea and prosperity in business.
N
Notable shrine Hanazono Jinja Hanazono Jinja

Guardian shrine of Shinjuku. Golden Gai and Kabukicho are next door. Red-tent theatre still continues in the grounds.
N
Notable shrine Atago Jinja Atago Jinja

It stands on Mount Atago, the highest natural hill in Tokyo's 23 wards. The 86 Stone Steps of Success are known for the story of a rider who climbed them on horseback.
N
Notable shrine Yoyogi Hachimangu Yoyogi Hachimangu

Just west of Meiji Jingu. The grounds preserve a Jomon settlement site and a reconstructed dwelling.
2

Lv2 · One hour by train (Yamanote loop + beyond)

Fuchu to the west. Ichikawa to the east. Yokohama to the south. Omiya to the north. A ring around central Tokyo.

N
Notable shrine Okunitama Jinja Okunitama Jinja (west · Fuchu)

The soja of Musashi Province. The Baba Daimon zelkova avenue is a National Natural Monument.
N
Notable shrine Katsushika Hachimangu Katsushika Hachimangu (east · Ichikawa)

The Sembon Ginkgo stands here. It is said to be 1,200 years old and is a National Natural Monument.
N
Notable shrine Iseyama Kotai Jingu Iseyama Kotai Jingu (south · Yokohama)

"The Ise of Kanto". The Meiji government built the shrine on a hill overlooking Yokohama harbour.
I
Ichinomiya Musashi Ichinomiya Hikawa Jinja Musashi Ichinomiya Hikawa Jinja (north · Omiya)

The head shrine of about 280 Hikawa shrines in Kanto. The place name Omiya is said to come from this shrine.
N
Notable shrine Chiba Jinja Chiba Jinja

Its "two-storey shrine" has a separate worship hall on each floor. The shrine follows Myoken worship of the North Star.
N
Notable shrine Fuda Tenjinja Fuda Tenjinja (Chofu)

An old shrine listed in the 927 Engishiki Jinmyocho. It is the guardian shrine of Chofu and is also associated with GeGeGe no Kitaro.
3

Lv3 · Half a day (fully suburban)

Kamakura, Chichibu, Okutama, Tanzawa, Ibaraki, Chiba and Kanagawa. A longer stay opens this outer ring.

I
Ichinomiya Tsurugaoka Hachimangu Tsurugaoka Hachimangu (Kamakura)

Centre of the Kamakura shogunate. Dankazura, the straight approach from the sea to the shrine, still forms the spine of the town. It calls itself the ichinomiya of Sagami Province. The traditional ichinomiya is Samukawa Jinja, also in this ring.
N
Notable shrine Zeniarai Benzaiten Zeniarai Benzaiten (Kamakura)

Money washed in the spring inside a rock-cut cave is said to multiply.
N
Notable shrine Egara Tenjinsha Egara Tenjinsha (Kamakura)

Counted among Japan's three great Tenjin shrines. A different pace from the crowds at Tsurugaoka Hachimangu.
N
Notable shrine Mitsumine Jinja Mitsumine Jinja (Chichibu)

About 1,100 metres up in the mountains. Wolves, or mountain dogs, stand where komainu usually would.
N
Notable shrine Musashi Mitake Jinja Musashi Mitake Jinja (Okutama)

On a 929-metre summit. The old oshi pilgrim lodgings still form a living settlement on the mountain.
N
Notable shrine Oyama Afuri Jinja Oyama Afuri Jinja (Tanzawa)

The "rain mountain". About 200,000 people a year are said to have climbed Oyama on pilgrimage in the Edo period.
J
Jingu Kashima Jingu Kashima Jingu (Ibaraki)

Ichinomiya of Hitachi Province, enshrining a god from the transfer-of-the-land myth. Its Kaname-ishi is said to pin down the giant catfish that causes earthquakes.
J
Jingu Katori Jingu Katori Jingu (Chiba)

Ichinomiya of Shimosa Province, enshrining the god sent with Kashima's deity in the transfer-of-the-land myth. Its Kaname-ishi forms a convex-and-concave pair with Kashima's.
I
Ichinomiya Samukawa Jinja Samukawa Jinja (Kanagawa)

Ichinomiya of Sagami Province. It is said to be Japan's only shrine for happo-yoke, protection from misfortune in every direction.
