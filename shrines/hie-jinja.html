History

Origins and founding

Guardian shrine of Edo Castle, now the Imperial Palace. Ota Dokan is said to have brought the deity from Kawagoe. It later became the tutelary shrine of the Tokugawa shoguns. It is a Chokusai shrine and was formerly a Kanpei Taisha.

Highlights

What to see

The Sanno torii, with the triangular top distinctive to Sanno worship / an escalator to the shrine buildings / monkeys as divine messengers, linked by wordplay to masaru, "evil departs" / rows of vermilion torii on the Inari approach.

A shrine building with "Sanno Yume Goten" on its plaque. High-rise buildings press close behind it.

A tunnel of vermilion torii leads to Sanno Inari shrine.

A wisteria trellis covers the corridor. Green leaves still wrap the whole passage after the flowers have gone.

Background

Cultural context

The head shrine is Hiyoshi Taisha in Shiga, regarded as the guardian of Mount Hiei. It is a standard example of Shinto-Buddhist syncretism (→ article "Japan and shrines"). The Sanno Matsuri is counted among Japan's three great festivals.
