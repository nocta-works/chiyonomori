History

Origins and founding

The ichinomiya, or first-ranked shrine, of Shinano Province. It is the head shrine of the Suwa shrines, said to number about ten thousand across Japan (according to the shrine). Its founding goes back to the transfer-of-the-land myth in the Kojiki, and it is regarded as one of the oldest shrines in Japan (according to the shrine).

Four shrines

The four shrines

South of Lake Suwa stand Honmiya and Maemiya, the Upper Shrine; to the north stand Harumiya and Akimiya, the Lower Shrine. Together the four are Suwa Taisha. The deities differ by shrine: Takeminakata at Honmiya, Yasakatome at Maemiya, and Takeminakata, Yasakatome and Yae Kotoshironushi at Harumiya and Akimiya (according to the shrine).

Background

Cultural context

A shrine with no main sanctuary. Honmiya, Harumiya and Akimiya have no main sanctuary: the Upper Shrine takes a mountain as its sacred object, and the Lower Shrine a sacred tree (a cedar at Harumiya, a yew at Akimiya). It is an old form of belief that worships nature itself (according to the shrine). (→ Torii and shrine grounds)

The Onbashira. The Shikinen Zoei Onbashira Taisai is held every seventh year, in the years of the Tiger and the Monkey (according to the shrine). The next is in 2028 (Reiwa 10, a Monkey year).

The deity Takeminakata is the god who resisted to the end in the transfer-of-the-land myth. He is said to have been defeated and fled to Shinano (→ Kami, ranks and lineages: the skeleton of the myths).
