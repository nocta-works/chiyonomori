History

Origins and founding

It enshrines Konohanasakuyahime and others. It stands on the northern side of Mount Fuji, at the start of the Yoshida climbing route. Where Fujisan Hongu Sengen Taisha to the south is a shrine that calms the mountain, this one grew up as a shrine for climbing it. Its signboard lists, as the deities of the main sanctuary, Konohanasakuyahime, Hikoho-no-ninigi (her husband) and Oyamatsumi (her father).

Highlights

What to see

The cedar avenue along the approach, said to be several hundred years old. The wooden great torii. The main hall and the East and West shrines. The Fuji climbing gate: once you pass through the approach, the climbing route begins. Worship and climbing are one continuous path.

According to the signboards, the main sanctuary, worship hall and offering hall were built in 1615 (Genna 1) by Torii Naritsugu, the lord of the Gunnai region, and were repaired and rebuilt over the years with the help of the Fuji-ko groups (Important Cultural Property). The Fuji Taro Sugi was the first natural monument designated by Yamanashi Prefecture (in 1958), and is about 30 metres tall. The grounds also hold a great cypress called the “Fuji Meoto-hinoki”, the Fuji married-couple cypress, because two cypresses have grown into one at the root and join again about 12 metres above the ground (a natural monument designated by Fujiyoshida City).

Background

Cultural context

The Fuji-ko. In the Edo period, ordinary people formed ko (savings groups) to climb Mount Fuji, and this was the gateway. Faith and travel began here as something inseparable.
